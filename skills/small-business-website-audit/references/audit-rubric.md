# Audit rubric

## Customer clarity

- Identify the business, offer, audience, location or service area, and distinguishing value within the first screen.
- Use concrete service names and outcomes instead of vague slogans alone.
- Keep navigation labels predictable and important information reachable without guesswork.

## Conversion

- Give the primary action a descriptive label and consistent placement.
- Provide the information needed before commitment: scope, price guidance when appropriate, process, availability, location, and next step.
- Ensure calls, forms, bookings, purchases, or donations work without dead ends.

## Accessibility

- Check landmarks, heading hierarchy, link purpose, image alternatives, labels, instructions, errors, contrast, keyboard operation, focus, reflow, motion, and media alternatives.
- Flag missing attributes as confirmed only when the DOM proves they are absent.
- Treat contrast from screenshots or computed values as a warning until colors and states are verified.
- Require manual assistive-technology testing before claiming conformance.

## Local SEO and discoverability

- Check unique titles and descriptions, crawlable service/location copy, canonical URLs, descriptive internal links, indexability, structured data relevance, and consistent business identity.
- Do not promise rankings or recommend location pages without useful unique content.

## Trust and content

- Check contact details, real policies, credentials, authorship, dates, testimonials, portfolio evidence, service boundaries, and claim support.
- Flag sample or fictional information if a public page could mislead visitors.

## Mobile and technical quality

- Check responsive navigation, tap targets, horizontal overflow, text resizing, image dimensions, loading behavior, broken resources, HTTPS, forms, and visible error states.
- Distinguish lab observations from real-user performance data.
